The Honest Comparison

You can get a website for $999. Here's what actually happens.

I'd rather say this plainly than have you find it out afterward. There are cheaper ways to get a website than hiring me, and for some businesses one of them is the right call. But the price you're comparing isn't the cost you'll pay.

The self-serve route
  • You write the copy. This is where most projects die — not in design, but in the six weeks the owner spends not writing the About page.
  • You get a login. You will use it twice, then forget the password.
  • Your hours change. You open a ticket, or you re-learn the editor, or the site stays wrong.
  • Templates ship generic markup. AI assistants can't tell what you do or where you are, so they recommend someone else.
  • Support is a queue in another timezone, answered by whoever is on shift.
  • In year two the subscription renews whether the site earned anything or not.
Working with me
  • I write the copy. You talk to me for an hour about what you actually do; I turn that into the site.
  • There is no login. There is nothing for you to learn, and nothing to forget.
  • Your hours change, you email me, and it's live the same day. That's the whole process.
  • Full structured data on every page, so ChatGPT and Google know what you do and which towns you serve.
  • You email me. I'm in Vanderhoof, not a call centre in another province.
  • You own the domain, the hosting account, and the files. Nothing is held hostage.

The real question isn't $999 versus $3,500. It's whether you want a website or whether you want a second job maintaining one. If you have the time and you enjoy that sort of thing, the cheap option is genuinely fine and I'll tell you so on the phone.

Most business owners I meet don't want a tool. They want it handled.
